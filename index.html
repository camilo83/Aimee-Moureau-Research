02 · Research objective

What the website needs to solve.

01 · Get understood

Explain the house in seconds.

A new visitor should quickly understand what Aimée Moureau makes, who it is for, where it sits in the market, and why it feels different from a generic formalwear store.

02 · Build confidence

Reduce the risk around an important purchase.

Fit, legitimacy, quality, occasion, product detail and service all need proof. Luxury cannot rely on atmosphere alone.

03 · Create a clear next step

No beautiful dead ends.

Every look should lead naturally to one of two useful actions: inquire about the piece or find where to see and buy it through a stockist.

04 · Buyer mindset

The client is not only choosing a dress. She is reducing risk.

01

Fit certainty.

She needs to understand silhouette, proportions, sizing and possible modifications before she feels ready to contact.

02

Occasion certainty.

The piece has to feel appropriate for a meaningful event, family context and level of formality — not simply fashionable.

03

Brand certainty.

A newer name must quickly prove that the work, service and construction are real. Trust comes from evidence, not invented history.

04

Process certainty.

She needs to know what happens after she likes a look: where to find it, who to contact, what information to send and what happens next.

05 · Buyer questions

Turn objections into website requirements.

01

“Is this a legitimate luxury house?”

Show the atelier, real craftsmanship, production details, close product photography, New York presence and a confident About page. The proof should feel specific, not promotional.

02

“Will this work on me?”

Each look should explain silhouette, fabric, colors, fit notes and possible modifications. A clear size guide and access to personal guidance reduce uncertainty.

03

“How do I buy it?”

Do not make the visitor guess. Every look should offer a direct inquiry tied to that piece, plus a clear route to authorized stockists.

04

“What happens after I contact you?”

Explain the next step in one short line: the team follows up, confirms availability, measurements or modifications, and guides the order.

05

“Can I see it in person?”

A dedicated Stockists / Where to Find Us path can make physical availability part of the experience instead of hiding it inside Contact.

06

“How much is it?”

This remains the key strategic decision before design. If prices stay hidden, the website must make the inquiry path feel deliberate and easy — not like missing information.

06 · Competitive benchmark

Borrow the logic, not the look.

Safiyaa

Service is visible before the sale.

Safiyaa makes personal consultation, customization and private showroom access part of the website experience. The service is not hidden in a footer.

For Aimée: make expert guidance a product benefit, not a back-office process.

Monique Lhuillier

Product and purchase logic are explicit.

Product pages, pricing, store locator, sizing information and made-to-order distinctions answer practical questions without weakening the luxury positioning.

For Aimée: clarity can feel premium. It does not need to feel transactional.

Marchesa

Occasion is a useful way to browse.

Filters include evening wear, formal, mother of the bride, wedding guest, black tie, silhouette, fabric and more.

For Aimée: we can tag looks by occasion and fit without forcing separate collections before the assortment needs them.

Teri Jon

Fit support is close to the product.

Detailed size guidance and access to a personal stylist reduce one of the biggest reasons to hesitate with formalwear.

For Aimée: size and fit support should sit beside the look, not in a disconnected help section.

07 · Product-first catalog

Give the garments more room.

Two columns, not four.

This is a design hypothesis to test in Figma, not a finished layout. The goal is simple: let each garment feel important enough to stop on. A dense four-column grid makes premium pieces behave like inventory.

  • 2-column editorial grid on desktop; 1 column on mobile.
  • Large image area with a second/detail image available quickly.
  • Minimal metadata in the grid; deeper details on the look page.
  • Use real texture, beading, fabric and construction as proof.
  • No sale language, discount badges or marketplace behavior.

Why this matters: Baymard found 56% of participants began a product page by exploring images. Aimée's own brand manual also gives white space, texture and craftsmanship a central role.

10 · Homepage logic

What the homepage should answer, in order.

01

What is Aimée Moureau?

A high-end New York eveningwear house for important occasions. Clear enough to understand in the first few seconds.

02

Why should I trust it?

Craftsmanship, precision, production, personal guidance and real visual proof. Luxury should be demonstrated before it is described.

03

What does the work look like?

Bring the garments forward early. The product is the strongest evidence of the brand.

04

Is there something right for my event?

Let visitors browse looks with useful tags such as occasion, silhouette, color or fabric when the assortment supports it.

05

How can I get it?

Explain both routes clearly: inquire directly about the look or find an authorized stockist.

06

What should I do next?

End with one calm, obvious action — not several competing calls to action.

Luxury should feel like confidence, not friction.

11 · Conversion system

Three clear paths. No checkout required.

Path 01 · Specific look

“I want this piece.”

Explore → open a look.
Evaluate → images, fit, fabric, colors, details.
Inquire → form automatically references the look number.
Follow-up → availability, measurements, modifications, order guidance.
Path 02 · In-person / retailer

“Where can I find it?”

Explore → understand the house and product.
Find a stockist → clear location list.
Choose a location → address / contact details.
Continue offline → visit or contact the retailer.
Path 03 · General question

“I need guidance first.”

Contact → short general form.
Context → occasion and basic request.
Guidance → team helps identify the right next step.
Outcome → look, stockist or direct order path.
Before design · One final decision

Do we definitely want to hide prices on every look?

A

Keep prices hidden

Supports a private sales process and keeps every inquiry inside the relationship. If we choose this route, the contact action must be immediate and the process must feel intentional.

B

Show prices

Lets clients self-qualify and removes a major source of uncertainty. Baymard's product-page research identifies price as one of the most important pieces of product information and finds unclear pricing can create distrust or abandonment.

This is not a recommendation to turn the site into ecommerce. It is a decision about transparency on a catalog page.
